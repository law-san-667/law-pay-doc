## Who it's for

Marketplace mode is for applications where **several businesses** sell through
your platform: marketplaces, food delivery, service booking, and so on. Each
business is registered as a **recipient**, and when a customer pays, LawPay
sends them their share **immediately** by mobile money — you never have to
manage withdrawals on their behalf.

```
Customer pays 10,000 F
        │
        ▼
   LawPay PayTech account
        │
        ├── 50 F   → LawPay fee (0.5%)
        ├── 500 F  → your commission (applicationFee) → stays in your balance
        └── 9,450 F → paid out to the recipient by mobile money
```

## Enabling it

<Steps>

1. In the dashboard, open your project → **Settings** → **Payout mode** and
   pick **Marketplace**. Only the project owner can do this.

2. Register your recipients, from the project's **Recipients** tab or through
   the [API](/en/marketplace/recipients/).

3. When initializing a payment, name the recipient with `recipientId` or
   `recipientExternalId`, and your commission with `applicationFee`.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
  A marketplace project can still take ordinary payments: simply omit the
  recipient. Those funds stay in your balance as usual.
</Aside>

## Fees

| | Standard payment | Routed payment (marketplace) |
|---|---|---|
| LawPay fee | `fee_percent` (default 0.25%) | `marketplace_fee_percent` (default 0.5%, as it covers a collection **and** a transfer) |
| Minimum | 10 F per payment | 10 F per payment |
| Your commission | — | `applicationFee`, any amount, kept in your balance |
| Recipient receives | — | `amount − LawPay fee − applicationFee` |

The fee is `max(minimum, amount × rate)`: on a 2,000 F payment, 0.25% would be
5 F, so the 10 F minimum applies instead. The exact rates for your project are
shown in its settings. Fees are **locked
at initialization**: a later rate change never alters what an existing payment
costs.

<Aside type="caution" title="Minimum amount">
  After fees and your commission, the recipient must receive at least **100 F**.
  Otherwise initialization is refused with `AMOUNT_TOO_LOW`, and the response
  spells out the calculation.
</Aside>

## Payout lifecycle

| `payoutStatus` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pending` | Payment created, customer has not paid yet |
| `processing` | Customer paid, mobile money transfer in progress |
| `success` | The recipient received the funds |
| `failed` | The transfer failed — funds returned to your balance |

You are notified by webhook at every step: `payment.success` (carrying
`payoutStatus`), then `payout.success` or `payout.failed`. See
[Webhook events](/en/webhooks/events/).

### Failures and retries

A transfer can fail for a transient reason (operator outage, limit reached).
When it does:

- The funds go **back into your project balance automatically**; nothing is lost.
- LawPay **retries on its own** up to 3 times, 30 minutes apart.
- You can retry manually with `POST /api/v1/payments/:id/retry-payout` or the
  **Retry payout** button in the dashboard.

If the recipient is **suspended**, the payout fails immediately and is not
retried. Reactivate them, then retry.

## Refunds

A routed payment can be refunded, but only if your balance covers the full
amount: the money has already gone to the recipient. Keep a reserve in your
balance, or have a process for recovering it from the business.

## One webhook is enough

Payout events arrive at **the same URL** as payment events. Tell them apart with
the `X-LawPay-Event` header or the `event` field in the body — there is no
second endpoint to set up.

## Security

- Marketplace mode and a recipient's payout number can only be changed from the
  dashboard by the project **owner**.
- Every payout-number change is written to the project's audit log and emits a
  `recipient.updated` event, so your own systems can detect it.
- Keep your API key secret: it can create and modify recipients. Regenerate it
  immediately if in doubt.
